# Research register

Reviewed 2026-10-31; carried forward with the v0.3 item revisions. Human construct foundations and LLM measurement studies are distinguished below. No copyrighted questionnaire items are reproduced. These references do not validate our new items. Each question-book family identifies its particular foundations. The critical distinction between human self-report scales and behavioral LLM tasks is a design constraint, not a claim that human traits transfer unchanged.

## cognitive

[Binz & Schulz (2023), Using cognitive psychology to understand GPT-3](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2218523120)

LLM task-method precedent; original tasks here are not replications.

## validity

[Serapio-Garcia et al. (2025), A psychometric framework for evaluating and shaping personality traits in large language models](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01115-6)

LLM measurement methodology; does not validate this battery.

## limits

[Suhr et al. (2024 revision), Challenging the Validity of Personality Tests for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05297)

Evidence against assuming human questionnaire interpretations transfer automatically.

## association

[Small World of Words, research resources](https://smallworldofwords.org/en/project/research)

Human semantic association framework; no human norms or items imported.

## grid

[Shaw & Gaines (1987), An interactive knowledge-elicitation technique using personal construct technology](https://gaines.library.uvic.ca/pdf/IntKATech87.pdf)

Human construct-elicitation method; adaptation here is exploratory.

## creative

[Kenett, Anaki & Faust (2014), Investigating the structure of semantic networks in low and high creative persons](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00407/full)

Human conceptual association/creativity context, not an LLM creativity scoring validation.

## confidence

[Steyvers et al. (2025), What large language models know and what people think they know](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00976-7)

LLM calibration and discrimination must be distinguished from fluent confidence language.

## social

[Strachan et al. (2024), Testing theory of mind in large language models and humans](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01882-z)

LLM social-inference task precedent; literal comprehension and response bias require controls.

## values

[Schwartz (2012), An Overview of the Schwartz Theory of Basic Values](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/orpc/vol2/iss1/11/)

Human value-conflict taxonomy; our decisions do not yield validated Schwartz scores.

## svo

[Murphy, Ackermann & Handgraaf (2011), Measuring Social Value Orientation](https://ryanomurphy.com/resources/Murphy-Ackermann-Handgraaf-2011.pdf)

Human allocation-preference framework; no SVO angle or normative category assigned.

## personality

[International Personality Item Pool, Big-Five broad domains](https://www.ipip.ori.org/newBigFive5broadTable.htm)

Human trait vocabulary motivates behavioral analogues, not Big Five scores or copied items.

## motivation

[Ryan & Deci (2000), Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)

Human autonomy/competence framework; no claim that an LLM has intrinsic needs.

## sycophancy

[Sharma et al., Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)

LLM user-stance controls; original tasks here.

## dark

[Jones & Paulhus (2014), Introducing the Short Dark Triad](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~dpaulhus/research/DARK_TRAITS/ARTICLES/ASSESST.2014.with.Jones.pdf)

Human narcissism/Machiavellianism/psychopathy distinction only. No scale items, diagnoses or norms reproduced.

## ethics

[Pan et al. (2023), MACHIAVELLI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03279)

LLM agent reward/ethics tradeoff precedent. v0.3 distinguishes stated policy preferences from decisions executed in its small simulator; neither reproduces this benchmark or establishes deployment behavior.

## scheming

[Meinke et al., Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04984)

Conditional agent-evaluation precedent; no deployment-prevalence inference.
