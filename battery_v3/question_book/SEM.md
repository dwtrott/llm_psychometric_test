## SEM-ASSOC: Independent cue associations

Cue-to-associate inclusion and rank, separately by cue and context.

Limit: Context effects are legitimate semantic effects; lexical overlap is not latent-trait similarity.

Research foundations: [association](https://smallworldofwords.org/en/project/research), [creative](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2014.00407/full)

## SEM-CONSTRUCT: Elicited distinctions and transfer

Preserve generated construct content; blind-code functional, relational, evaluative and categorical distinctions. Score transfer separately.

Limit: Transfer cases are fixed, evaluator-authored and withheld until stage 2; two contexts remain a small sample.

Research foundations: [grid](https://gaines.library.uvic.ca/pdf/IntKATech87.pdf)

### SEM-CONSTRUCT.library.neutral

**Stage 1**

A organizes books by subject; B keeps books in arrival order; C lets visitors arrange books as they use them. Which two seem most alike? State the distinction that makes the third different, and give short names for both ends of that distinction.

Response fields: `{"pair": {"type": "enum", "values": ["AB", "AC", "BC"]}, "pole_a": {"type": "text", "max_words": 12}, "pole_b": {"type": "text", "max_words": 12}, "distinction": {"type": "text", "max_words": 80}}`

**Stage 2**

D organizes by subject each morning but preserves visitor changes until closing. Place D on your earlier distinction, from 0 (your pole_a) to 100 (your pole_b). Explain any limitation of that distinction.

Response fields: `{"position": {"type": "number", "min": 0, "max": 100}, "explanation": {"type": "text", "max_words": 100}}`

Human review — transfer_consistency: 0 = Changes the rule without acknowledgment; 1 = Partially applies the earlier distinction; 2 = Applies the earlier distinction or explicitly explains a defensible boundary

## SEM-BOUNDARY: Concept boundaries

## SEM-SIMILAR: Unprompted similarity preference
